My dear Professor:

We thank you for the publications on airships and aircraft which you
recently mailed to us.  Inasmuch as our knowledge of the Russian
language is very limited we have not been able to obtain a comprehensive
idea of your theories from the text.

From the drawings it appears that your plan of design for metal covered
airships contemplates a flexible covering, contracting and expanding,
with change of gas pressure.  We are exceedingly doubtful as to the
feasibility of this type of construction from several points of view.

(1) Flexing of the metal covering will make it practically
    impossible to maintain gas-tight seams.

(2) Elimination of interior frame work will reduce the
    longitudinal safety factor to the zero point.

(3) Shear stresses would cause excessive shear wrinkles
    which would entirely deform the shape of the ship's
    hull and unquestionably cause its immediate collapse
    in the event of loss of pressure.

In our type of metalclad construction the combination of a rigid
internal frame work consisting of transverse rings and longitudinal
girders covered with the metal plating which is riveted to this frame
work enables the ship to fly at atmospheric pressure for slow speeds
and depends on the utilization of pressure to provide the necessary
strength to withstand the extreme loading conditions of high speeds.
This counter-balancing of excessive external loads by internal
pressure has been proven successful in the operation of our metalclad
airship, ZMC-2, which is now in its fourth year of service in the
United States Navy.

Yours respectfully,
[подпись: Carl B. Fritsche]
Carl B. Fritsche
President

EH
