# Fond 555 in dates

Files in the fond: 2,019. Dated: 1,971 (98%). Conjecturally dated: 157 (8%).

The archive's portal carries a date on every file's card, but only 46 of the 2,019 file descriptions state a year in the title itself. Collected here into a table, the fond can be read chronologically as a whole. Titles and dates are quoted throughout as the archive records them, in Russian and in the archive's own date format.

The last four years of his life, 1932-1935, account for 225 files of 568, that is 40%, and 7,444 sheets of 27,954, that is 27%.

The two shares diverge, and the gap matters: late files are markedly shorter than early ones. The work does not stop, it changes form, moving from extended treatises to short notes. The sheets-per-file column rests on four files each for the 1870s and 1880s and means nothing on its own; the decline is visible from the 1900s on, where the counts run to tens and hundreds.

## Variant number against chronology

Some works are held as several files marked 1st and 2nd variant. The variant number and the order of writing do not always agree.

| Work | File | Variant | Dates as described |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Воздушный корабль | no. 127 | 1 | 20.02.1907 |
| Воздушный корабль | no. 130 | 2 | [1907 г.] |
| Есть ли бог? | no. 490 | 1 | 1932 г. |
| Есть ли бог? | no. 491 | 2 | март 1932 г. |
| Космический корабль | no. 46 | 1 | июль 1924 г. - 8.06.1926 |
| Космический корабль | no. 47 | 2 | [июнь 1925 г.] |
| Ступени человечества и преобразование земли | no. 413 | 1 | 19.10.1920 |
| Ступени человечества и преобразование земли | no. 414 | 2 | 1.09.1920 |

For «Ступени человечества и преобразование земли» the second variant is dated earlier than the first: file 414 is 1.09.1920, while file 413 is 19.10.1920. Both dates are firm, with no square brackets.

The practical consequence: the 1st variant cannot be cited as the earlier one, the order has to be taken from the dating.

## The response to the notice "Who saw the bolide?"

A bolide crossed the Moscow region on 14 May 1934. Tsiolkovsky placed a notice in the newspapers asking eyewitnesses to write to him. The replies entered the fond as separate files, one per correspondent: 218 letters, 920 sheets, all in opis 4.

| Month | Letters |
|---|---:|
| 1934-06 | 196 |
| 1934-07 | 20 |

Of the 218 letters, 216 are dated to the month or better. The response is over within weeks: the peak month holds 196 letters, 91% of all dated ones.

The file descriptions name the correspondents with their occupations: a physics teacher at an agricultural college, the head of quality control at a factory, a foreman at a chemical works, a livestock specialist, a magazine editor, a village council. This is an observational campaign run by a private individual through the newspapers, reaching people with no connection to science.
